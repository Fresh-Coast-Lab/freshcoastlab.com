January 13, 2026MSP 3.0

“Add AI” is not a strategy.

What I told the Partnerships Unraveled podcast about the MSP of the future.

I sat down with Efe Senel on the Partnerships Unraveled podcast to talk about what I call MSP 3.0. The short version: the MSPs who win the next few years won't be the ones with the most AI in their pitch deck.

AI is a differentiator. It is not a strategy. If your plan is “add AI,” you don't have a plan, you have a feature. The strategy is still the same one it has always been: know your customer's business better than anyone, and make their technology boring in the best way.

Three things I keep coming back to

Open beats walled. Partners don't want to be locked into one vendor's garden. They want tools that play well with the stack they already run. Every time I see an MSP get boxed in, it costs them later.

Simple wins. Enablement and licensing should not require a decoder ring. If a partner can't explain what they're selling in one breath, their customer won't buy it.

There's money on the table. A lot of MSPs are sitting on vendor program revenue they never claim, especially in the Microsoft world. That's not a technology problem. That's a “nobody looked” problem.

Where AI actually fits

AI earns its keep when it makes your people faster and your service better. Summarizing tickets. Drafting the QBR. Catching the alert nobody was watching. That's real. What isn't real is slapping “AI-powered” on the same service and raising the price.

Build the strategy first. Then let AI make it hard to compete with.
