Build log

How this site was built.

One person, Claude Code, and a small team of AI agents, over about a day. I set the direction and approved every change. The agents did the typing, the testing and a lot of the arguing. Here’s what that actually looked like.

The team

Me

Direction, taste, and the final yes. Every commit went through a secret scan and a human look before it shipped.

Claude Code

The main pair: wrote and refactored the site, ran the browser tests, and kept a running list of everything still open.

Research agents

Studied what the best mobile storytelling sites do, then a 100-agent fact-check on whether 3D holds up on mid-range phones.

A 3D builder

Modeled the house in Blender, baked its lighting, and wrote the three.js engine with its own performance monitor.

Reviewers

An adversarial code reviewer and a test-matrix agent tried to break the house on every screen size, network failure and lifecycle edge.

Collectors

Read my LinkedIn history (read-only) to rebuild a timeline of talks and awards, and inventoried every tool I actually use.

Things the agents caught before you did

  1. Black squares in the sky.The 3D glow shaders wrote solid alpha, so every light in the yard punched a dark tile through the aurora. Fixed with premultiplied alpha.
  2. One bad moment, banned for life.A dropped connection or a phone switching apps could mark a device as “can’t do 3D” forever. Now only a measured struggle counts, and it expires.
  3. A 1,051-pixel house on a 390-pixel screen.Turn a phone sideways and the house grew taller than the screen. The test matrix found it; the stage now fits.
  4. A sign that vanished in Safari.Four stacked glow effects made WebKit silently drop the whole image. Chrome rendered it fine, which is exactly why we test in Safari’s engine.
  5. A “g” that wasn’t cut off.Two CSS fixes did nothing, because nothing was clipping it: the original font draws a short tail. Rendering it at triple line height proved it. New typeface.
  6. A stat that wasn’t true.A draft cited Gartner for a number Gartner never published. Checked, sourced, and corrected to the study that did.

How it’s made

  • Hand-written HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No framework, no bundler.
  • A live WebGL2 shader for the sky, driven by Traverse City’s real sun, wind and Lake Michigan waves.
  • three.js with lighting baked in Blender for the 3D house, and an SVG fallback for devices that can’t carry it.
  • Cloudflare Workers: the site, a weather endpoint cached at the edge, and per-branch preview URLs.
  • gitleaks before every commit. A human approves every push to production.

The point isn’t that AI built a website. It’s that one person with good agents, clear standards and a habit of checking the work can ship something this careful in a day. That’s the same thing I tell businesses about AI.
